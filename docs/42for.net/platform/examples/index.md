# Examples

The same seven annotations describe products that look nothing alike. What changes is the meaning of **subject**, **context**, and **responsibility**. Usage, execution, and configuration stay the mechanism that keeps every variant understandable.

These six are modeled end to end. The first two are the setups a software house actually operates: a B2B product that is a little different for every customer, and a consumer finance suite where one person acts as several legal entities. The others use the same model for a large distributed platform, a monolith with several versions still in production, a physical product and its parts, and a fleet of devices.

| Example | Subject | Context | Responsibility |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| [B2B SaaS](b2b-saas) | A business customer | An environment or instance | A module or service |
| [B2C finance suite](b2c-finance) | A person | The legal entity they represent | A financial product |
| [Distributed platform](distributed-platform) | A market | A cell | A service |
| [Versioned monolith](versioned-monolith) | A major version still in production | A channel pinned to that version | A module inside the process |
| [Physical product](physical-product) | A product model | A trim, a plant, or a vehicle | A subsystem or part family |
| [Device fleet](device-fleet) | A site | A device | A capability on the device |

Each page has the catalog, the keys, one configuration story, and the questions the model answers on a normal day. Read [Annotations](../annotations) first if the words are new, and [How to model](../modeling) if you are about to name your own.
