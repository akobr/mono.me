## Important

1. **The fallback `mcpBudgetAmount` differs between docs: 7 in the spec, 11 elsewhere.** `docs/Platform/Deployment/specs/2026-11-22 Cost Range and Budget of the MCP Server.md` ("Why amount 1 and these thresholds") says "the same thresholds work with `mcpBudgetAmount = 7`". `decisions.md`, runbook phase 11 ("A cost although the replica-minutes are inside the grant") and the PR description say `11`. With a grant worth about 5.40 USD, 7 puts the 50 % email (3.50) *inside* the grant, so it would fire while everything is still free. 11 is the value that keeps "the first email means the grant is used up". **Fix:** change the spec to 11 (or mark it superseded) so the spec and the runbook agree.
2. **The amount is in the billing currency, but the fallback is worked out in USD.** `main.bicep` `mcpBudgetAmount` (description: "in its billing currency") and runbook phase 11 say so. But "11 = just past a 5.40 USD grant" holds only if the subscription bills in USD. If it bills in EUR or SEK, 1 unit still works as "any cost at all". The 11 advice, the "0.50 USD ≈ 19 warm hours" in the spec, and the 50 %/100 % meanings would all be off, by about 10× in SEK. **Fix:** say in phase 11 and the decisions which currency the subscription bills in. If it isn't USD, give the converted value, or phrase it as "about twice the grant's value in the billing currency".

## Nits

1. **`main.bicep`, `mcpBudgetStartDateChecked`:** "10 characters ending in `-01`" lets through `2026-13-01`, `2026/12-01` or `xxxxxxx-01`. ARM would refuse these later, at the budget PUT, with a less helpful message. A slightly stronger check is cheap in Bicep: also require `substring(d, 4, 1) == '-'` and `int(substring(d, 5, 2))` between 1 and 12. Not required, since the what-if still catches it before any write.
2. **The `fail()` check runs only at the what-if (documented in the review doc).** `Platform.Deployment.yml` won't catch `mcpEnabled = true` with an empty date in a PR. That is consistent with the other MCP checks. If you want it caught in CI, a decorator-only test is impossible here, so the only route would be a what-if job, which isn't worth it now.
3. **Runbook phase 11, step 1:** the day loop counts per UTC day from the 1st, while the cost data uses the billing period. That's fine for a rough count, but "this month" can be a few hours off around midnight UTC. Summing per-minute `Maximum` also slightly over-counts partial minutes, which is conservative and fine. Maybe add one line saying it's an upper estimate.
4. **Runbook phase 11, step 2** reuses `$app` and `$now` from step 1. Say "in the same session", or repeat the two lines, so the snippet can be pasted on its own.
5. **`modules/mcp.bicep` header** hard-codes "about 0.027 USD an hour" and "about 200 hours". These go stale if `mcpCooldownSeconds`, CPU or memory change. Consider pointing to `decisions.md` for the numbers and keeping only the mechanism in the comment.

## Good

- The cost is described honestly: a 401 or a scanner request still wakes a replica, and the host name is in the CT logs. Those are exactly the facts behind the old "0 USD" claim, and they are now in the template comments where the next reader will see them.
- The `cooldownPeriod` comment correctly says it governs only the last replica (1 → 0), with N → 1 left to the 300 s scale-down window.
- The budget's filter is the documented `MeterCategory In` form, it has three of the five allowed notifications, and it uses the same `GreaterThanOrEqualTo`/`ownerEmail` conventions as `prod-2s-budget`. The naming goes through `naming.bicep`.
- Phase 10 now resets `mcpBudgetStartDate` after a budget is deleted. That trap (a new budget can't start in a past month) is easy to miss.
- The open points (the meter category in cost data, and whether free usage is priced during the month) are stated, with a check in phases 9 and 11 for each, instead of being assumed.
