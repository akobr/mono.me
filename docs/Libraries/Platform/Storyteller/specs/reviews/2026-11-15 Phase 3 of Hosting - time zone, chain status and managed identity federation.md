# Phase 3 of Hosting: time zone, chain status and managed identity federation

Linear: [424-256](https://linear.app/424fun/issue/424-256/hosting-time-zone-chain-status-and-managed-identity-federation).

## Overview

Executes phase 3, the chain status in `WatchAsync` (section 2.5 for watches, with its parts of sections 4 and 5), of [Hosting: time zone, chain status and managed identity federation](../2026-11-12%20Hosting%20-%20time%20zone,%20chain%20status%20and%20managed%20identity%20federation.md). A watch of a target with `RequiringActiveAncestors()` now holds a descendant only while every ancestor of its chain is active. Every poll reads each distinct ancestor of the held and of the listed descendants once. A held descendant whose ancestor is inactive or gone leaves as `Removed`, with the ancestor's annotation key and its reason in `StorytellerRemoval`, and comes back as `Added` once all its ancestors are active. Everything is additive: a watch of a target without the requirement sends the requests it sent before, and no public member, message or `ToString()` text changed. `WatchExecutionsAsync` and `AddSubscriptions` keep their rule (2.6), and the federation is phase 4. A review of the change confirmed seven findings, none a defect of the behaviour. One changed the code: the loop that reads the distinct ancestors, which the lists and the informer each had a copy of, is now one method of `AncestorReader`. The other six changed a comment, `hosting.md` and tests. The change is `e2dea92b`.

## What Was Done

### 2.5 Targets that require active ancestors

Done for `WatchAsync`. The option of the target, its `ToString()` and the lists are phase 2.

- **The reads of a poll.** `Informer/DescendantPollingSource` has a new `ReadAncestorsAsync`, which a poll calls for a target whose scope has `RequireActiveAncestors`: after the list, before the diff. It takes the listed descendants that pass the filters and the held ones that are not listed, and reads each distinct ancestor of them once, in any status but deleted, through the poll's limiter of 4 reads at once.
- **A failed read is an answer.** The informer's read of an ancestor (`ReadAncestorAsync`) keeps a failure in its `AncestorRead`, so one failure leaves the other reads of the poll alone. `AncestorReader.ReadAsync` still throws any failure but a `403` `AnnotationRestricted` and a `404`, so who am I and the lists read as before.
- **The target's own ancestor.** `CheckAncestorAsync` now reads through `AncestorReader.ReadAsync` and returns its read. The request and the outcomes are the same: a `404` throws the failure of an ancestor that does not exist, a `403` `AnnotationRestricted` logs its Debug line and goes on to the list, and any other failure fails the poll. Until a poll has completed, that read serves for the target's own ancestor. After that the check is gone, and the ancestor is read with the others.
- **One rule with the lists.** `Reading/AncestorReader` gained `ChainOf` (the full keys of the ancestors in the chain order of phase 1) and `FindInactive` (the ancestor that keeps a key from being under active ancestors). `AreActive`, which the lists use, now calls `FindInactive`. `Reading/AncestorRead` gained `IsMissing` and `IsUndecided`.
- **The one chain order.** `StorytellerExecutionContext.ChainOrder` went from a private array to an internal `IReadOnlyList<AnnotationType>`, so that `ChainOf` uses it.
- **What `FindInactive` gives.** An ancestor whose read told nothing, restricted or failed, comes first. Otherwise it is the first one in the chain order that is missing, deleted or not active. An ancestor without a read counts as missing.
- **The ancestors of a poll.** The private record `PollAncestors` holds the reads by full key and one time. Its `RemovalBy` gives the `StorytellerRemoval` of an ancestor: the ancestor's annotation key and the reason of `PollReads.ReasonOf`, never with `includeInactive`. It gives `null` for an ancestor whose read told nothing.
- **Removed.** In `DiffAsync` a held descendant that is still listed leaves with the removal of its ancestor. It is not read, and it is not `Changed` in that poll, also when its fingerprint moved.
- **The descendant's own end goes first.** A held descendant that is not listed any more goes through `ConfirmAsync`, which reads it first, as in any watch. What that read tells (missing, deleted, or inactive unless the target includes inactive ones) names the descendant itself. An ancestor is named only when the descendant itself stays.
- **Added.** A listed descendant that is not held is read only when `FindInactive` gives none. Otherwise it is not read, and a Debug line names its ancestor. So the first poll adds only the descendants under active ancestors, and a later poll adds the others.
- **A failed ancestor read**, with any failure but a `404`, logs one warning per ancestor per poll, in the order of the keys. The descendants under that ancestor stay as they are: a held one is not removed for an ancestor and still follows its own diff, and one that is not held is neither added nor read.
- **A definitive failure before the first completed poll** is thrown from `ReadAncestorsAsync`, so it fails the poll as the other failures of a target do, and `WatchAsync` ends with it. A restricted ancestor is thrown as `DescendantScope.CannotReadAncestor`, the exception that a list throws since phase 2. After a completed poll it is logged as a failed ancestor read, with that message, and the watch polls again.
- **The order of events** did not change: `Removed`, then `Changed`, then `Added`, each by key. The removals for an ancestor and those of a descendant's own read are one group.
- **Cost.** One point read per distinct ancestor per poll, the first poll included: 1 + 1 + 7 + 3 requests for three executions of two subjects in three contexts, then 1 + 8 in every later poll.
- **`StorytellerRemoval`.** Its comment names the annotation that ended a descendant with the spec's words: "the descendant, or for a target that requires active ancestors, one of them". The record did not change.
- **The comments** of `StorytellerRemovalReason`, `StorytellerAnnotationChange`, `StorytellerChangeKind`, `IStorytellerCatalog.WatchAsync`, `StorytellerTarget<TAnnotation>.RequiringActiveAncestors()`, `PollReads.ReasonOf`, `StorytellerCatalog`, `AncestorReader`, `AncestorRead` and `DescendantPollingSource` say the same. Two of them said that a removal carries the annotation's own key, which no longer holds for every target.
- **Without the option** nothing changed. `ancestors` is `null` in the diff, no ancestor but the target's own is read, and the 31 tests that `WatchTests` had pass unchanged.
- **Left open by the spec: a failed read next to an inactive ancestor.** The spec says both that a held descendant under an inactive ancestor leaves, and that a failed ancestor read keeps the descendants that depend on it. Here the failed read wins: an ancestor whose read failed defers every removal by an ancestor of that descendant, also by a nearer one that was read as inactive. `FindInactive` returns the undecided read first, and `RemovalBy` gives `null` for it. So a transient failure never changes which ancestor is named. With an ancestor that stays restricted after a completed poll, the descendant under another, disabled ancestor stays held. The descendant's own end is still told.
- **Left open by the spec: a held descendant that the list missed, whose own read fails.** It stays for that poll, also under an inactive ancestor: `ConfirmAsync` returns before it asks the ancestors. Its own end goes first, and a failure is no answer about it.
- **Deviation: the target's own ancestor is read before the list in the first poll.** The spec reads the ancestors "after the list and before the diff". The read of the existence check is made before the list, and it serves until a poll has completed. A second read after the list would make two reads of one ancestor in that poll, against the spec's cost of one. The lists of phase 2 do the same.
- **Left open by the spec: a deleted ancestor.** An ancestor is read without deleted ones, which is what lets the existence check serve. A soft-deleted ancestor therefore answers `404` and gives `Missing`. `Deleted` is given only when a read answers a deleted snapshot. A deletion cascades, so in practice the descendant leaves with its own key and `Deleted`.
- **Left open by the spec: which definitive failure ends the watch.** The first one by ordinal full key, so the failure does not depend on the order in which the reads ended. One that is not a restriction, such as a `401` or a `403` `AccessDenied`, is thrown as the read's own exception.
- **Left open by the spec: a transient failure in the first poll** does not fail the poll. The descendants under that ancestor are not added, a warning is logged, and the poll completes. A definitive failure in a later poll is then only logged, as "after a completed poll" says.
- **Left open by the spec: the zero interval.** A definitive failure of an ancestor is thrown. A transient one leaves the descendants under that ancestor out, with the warning, as a failed point read of a descendant does in the one poll.
- **Left open by the spec: the time.** The time that tells the ancestors' status is taken once per poll, after their reads ended. A descendant's own status is told at its own read, as before.
- **Left open by the spec: the log texts.** The warning is "Reading {Key} failed ({Class}); the descendants under it stay as they were, and the next poll reads it again. {Error}", where the error of a restricted ancestor is the message of `CannotReadAncestor`. The Debug line is "{Key} is listed, but its ancestor {Ancestor} is {Status}; it is not added, and the next poll decides again.", with the removal reason as the status, or "not read".
- **A rule of phase 2 that moved.** `FindInactive` counts a snapshot with `DeletedAt` as not active, which `AreActive` did not ask before. The catalog cannot reach it: it reads the ancestors without deleted ones. A test double whose `GetAsync` answers a deleted snapshot, against its contract, can: the default `ListWithAncestorsAsync` of a target that requires active ancestors then leaves out the descendants under it.
- **`AncestorRead` can hold any failure now.** Only the informer makes such reads.
- **A watch whose first poll lists no descendants** reads no ancestor and completes that poll. A machine that may not read the ancestors of descendants that appear later gets a warning at every poll, not an exception.
- **One warning per unreadable ancestor per poll.** A restricted machine with many subjects logs that many warnings at every poll after the first completed one, as failed point reads are logged. They are not aggregated.
- **Fixed after review: the loop that reads the distinct ancestors was copied.** `DescendantPollingSource.ReadAncestorsAsync` repeated `StorytellerCatalog.ReadAncestorsAsync` almost line for line. The loop is now `AncestorReader.ReadDistinctAsync(keys, known, read)`: each distinct ancestor of the keys once, by full key, with an ancestor that the caller read already serving instead of a second read. The lists call it with their limited read that throws, and the informer with its own read that keeps a failure. The informer keeps what is its own after it: the failed reads, the definitive failure, the warnings and `PollAncestors`. `StorytellerCatalog.ListAsync` spells its existence check with `IsMissing`, as the informer does. The behaviour did not change.
- **Fixed after review: the comment of `StorytellerRemovalReason`** said that `Disabled`, `Scheduled` and `Expired` of an ancestor "always" end an annotation. That holds only for a target that requires active ancestors. It now says "those of an ancestor that the target requires active end it whether it includes inactive ones or not".

### Deviations

- No public member or signature differs from the spec.
- One rule differs from the spec's sentence: in the first poll the target's own ancestor is read before the list, not after it, to keep the cost of one read per ancestor.
- The spec left open what the sections above decide, with their reasons: a failed read next to an inactive ancestor, a failed read of a held descendant that the list missed, a deleted ancestor, which definitive failure ends the watch, a transient failure in the first poll, the zero interval, the time of the ancestors' status, the log texts, and the place of the new section in `hosting.md`.
- Beyond the spec: `AncestorReader.ReadDistinctAsync`, shared by the lists and the informer; the internal `ChainOrder`; `FindInactive` counting a deleted snapshot as not active, in the lists too; the Debug line of a descendant that is not added; and in the tests the nearest ancestor, the filters, the limiter, the cancellation, the zero interval and the sample of the docs.
